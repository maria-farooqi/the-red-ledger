Socialism is a left economic philosophy that first rose to prominence in early-to-mid-19th-century Europe. Its advocates generally seek to reduce economic inequality, limit the concentration of wealth and economic power, and strengthen workers' rights. Socialist economies can be divided into Planned Economies, which seek to eliminate the perceived inefficiencies, irrationalities, unpredictability, and crises that socialists traditionally associate with capital accumulation and the profit system, and Market Economies, which retain the use of monetary prices, factor markets and sometimes the profit motive.
